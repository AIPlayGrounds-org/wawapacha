# WawaPacha story

The site answers six questions in one reading path. Registry links identify the
entries that support each view.

## What is the central-Pacific signal?

The central signal uses
[enso-is-ocean-atmosphere-pattern](../evidence/claims.toml),
[oni-definition-and-threshold](../evidence/claims.toml),
[nino-region-definitions](../evidence/claims.toml),
[weekly-nino-anomalies](../evidence/claims.toml) and
[roni-is-current-official-index](../evidence/claims.toml). The ONI explorer
lives on `/aprende`; the homepage starts with the ENFEN hero, then shows the
sea-temperature map and weekly regional values.

## What is happening at the coast?

The coastal reading uses
[global-and-coastal-can-differ](../evidence/claims.toml),
[icen-definition](../evidence/claims.toml) and
[coastal-event-criterion](../evidence/claims.toml). After the ENFEN hero, the
homepage shows the sea-temperature map, weekly regional values and ICEN. The
historical route places the coastal index after the sea chronology.

The site makes no claim about how El Niño Costero rainfall differs between the
north and south coasts.

## What is the official state in Peru?

The official-state reading uses [enfen-alert-states](../evidence/claims.toml).
The homepage starts with the ENFEN hero, then shows the sea-temperature map,
weekly regional values and ICEN, historical comparison, rain comparison for the
earlier extraordinary events, and the authority outlook. Local territory, rivers
and archive links close the page.

## What do local observations show?

The local views use
[senamhi-hydrometeorological-stations](../evidence/claims.toml),
[chirps-rainfall-estimates](../evidence/claims.toml),
[chirps-pentad-latency](../evidence/claims.toml),
[era5-historical-reanalysis](../evidence/claims.toml) and
[era5-latency-and-limitations](../evidence/claims.toml). The homepage links to
`/territorio`, `/rios` and `/historico` after the outlook. The historical route
ends with station observations.

## What should the reader expect next?

The intensity outlook uses
[noaa-enso-strength-probabilities](../evidence/claims.toml) and
[noaa-enso-probability-meaning](../evidence/claims.toml). It follows the
historical comparison and the earlier extraordinary-event rain comparison on the
homepage, before the local links. The river view uses
[glofas-river-discharge-forecast](../evidence/claims.toml),
[glofas-ensemble-percentiles](../evidence/claims.toml) and
[glofas-river-point-limits](../evidence/claims.toml); the site keeps that view
separate from the ocean signal.

## What happened in past events?

The historical reading uses
[historical-coastal-chronology](../evidence/claims.toml). The homepage compares
past events before the authority outlook and ends with a link to `/historico`.
`/historico` shows the sea chronology first, the coastal index second and
station observations third.

## Reading path

The homepage presents the ENFEN hero, sea-temperature map, weekly regional
values and ICEN, historical comparison, earlier extraordinary-event rain
comparison, authority outlook and local links in that order. The historical
route presents the sea chronology, coastal index and station observations in
that order.
